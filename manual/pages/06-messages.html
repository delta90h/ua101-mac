Read the Messages window and its warnings

The Messages window tells you, in plain words, what each change did: whether it was sent, whether the box took it, and anything to look at.

  • Open it with View → Show Messages (⌥⌘M) or the Messages button at the top of the mixer.
  • It never opens by itself. Ordinary (grey) messages only go into the list; the number on the Messages button shows how many wait.

What is in it

  • At the top: the state, in full. Ready says what the app may change. The Lock Changes button there does the same as Device → Lock Changes; while changes are locked it says Unlock.
  • NOW: messages you haven't answered yet. Click OK on a message when you have read it; it moves down to EARLIER. OK to All answers them all.
  • EARLIER: the last 100 messages since the app opened. Clear Earlier empties the list.
  • Copy All copies the messages as text. Right-click one message for Copy This Message.
  • At the bottom: Export as CSV… saves the list as a file; Open the Full Record… opens the app's full record (every change since the first day); A− and A+ make the text smaller or larger.

Warnings

A message with a coloured frame is a warning. Read it before you go on.

A red warning comes up by itself in a small window of its own, UA-101 Warning. It tells you that something went wrong with a change that was already sent: read it, then click OK (or press Return). Nothing more is done; the message moves down to EARLIER in the Messages window. If more come at once, they come up one after another.

A volume held at 0 dB when you drag a fader to the top is normal: it only goes into the list.

  • The unit didn't take a change: the box holds another value than the one sent (the status light then says The UA-101 kept another value). The screen shows what the box really holds. Try the change again; if it happens again, save a report (Problems and questions).
  • Red words in a confirmation (before a Recall, Save, Open Backup or Reset) say what will be overwritten and that it can't be undone, except from a backup. See Safety habits.
