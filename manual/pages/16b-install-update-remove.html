Install, update and remove

You need a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Everything comes in one file, UA-101 Driver.pkg: the driver, the MIDI device and this app.

Install

  1. Quit the apps that play sound, and UA-101 Control if it is open. At the end of the install all sound and MIDI on the Mac stop for a moment.
  2. Double-click UA-101 Driver.pkg, follow the pages, click Install and type your Mac password.
  3. If macOS says it can't check the file and won't open it, click Done. Then open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security, click Open Anyway next to the installer's name, type your password and click Open Anyway again. You do this once for each downloaded copy: the installer is not signed through Apple's paid Developer ID program.
  4. macOS may say a background item was added: that is the driver's helper. Leave it allowed.
  5. Open UA-101 Control from Applications. With the box on, it says Ready.

The installer puts these on the Mac: UA-101 Control in Applications, the sound device and the MIDI device (in the Mac's Library folder), and the helper that talks to the box over USB. The driver is for every account on the Mac.

Install a newer version

Install the newer UA-101 Driver.pkg the same way, over the old one. You don't need to remove the old one first: the installer stops and replaces it.

  • Your backups, the box's settings and its five memories stay as they are.
  • Your Driver Settings stay too.
  • Afterwards, open UA-101 Control: it says Ready when all is well.

Remove the driver and the app

There are two ways. Choose one when you remove:

  • Remove the Driver, Keep My Settings: the driver, its MIDI device and UA-101 Control go. Your Driver Settings, the app's own choices (window size, appearance and so on) and its record stay, so a later install starts where you left off.
  • Remove Everything: the same, and then every UA-101 setting, record, log and crash report on the Mac, in every account. Nothing of the UA-101 is left.

Your own files are kept either way, unless you say otherwise: your backups in Documents → UA-101 Backups and your reports in Documents → UA-101 Reports. Nothing in the box is changed: its settings and memories stay.

From UA-101 Control (the easy way)

  1. Quit the apps that play sound. At the end all sound and MIDI on the Mac stop for a moment.
  2. Choose UA-101 Control → Uninstall UA-101…
  3. Click Remove the Driver, Keep My Settings or Remove Everything. With Remove Everything, the tick Keep my backup files and reports keeps your own files; untick it and they go too. Cancel changes nothing.
  4. macOS asks for an administrator's name and password, in its own window. Type them and click OK.
  5. A few seconds later a window says what was removed and what stayed. Click Quit: UA-101 Control closes, and it is gone from Applications. With Remove Everything, the grey UA-101 is gone from Audio MIDI Setup too (in your account).

From Terminal (if the app is already gone)

  1. Quit the apps that play sound, and UA-101 Control.
  2. Open Terminal (in Applications → Utilities) and type, with the quotes: 
    "/Library/Application Support/ua101-driver/uninstall.sh"
    then press Return.
  3. It asks which way: type 1 (keep my settings) or 2 (everything) and press Return. With 2 it asks about your own files: press Return to keep them, or type remove.
  4. Type yes and press Return, then type your Mac password (nothing shows while you type) and press Return.
  5. It ends with What happened: what was removed and what stayed.

The grey UA-101 in Audio MIDI Setup

macOS remembers every MIDI device it has seen. After the driver is removed, Audio MIDI Setup may show a grey UA-101 in MIDI Studio: it does nothing. With Remove the Driver, Keep My Settings it stays on purpose: it comes back to life when you install again. To remove it by hand (in another account, or after the Terminal way): open Audio MIDI Setup, choose Window → Show MIDI Studio, click the grey UA-101 and press Delete.
